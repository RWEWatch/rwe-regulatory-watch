## Methodology & scientific publications

### Target Trial Emulation Pitfalls: Immortal Time Bias Induced by Exposure Misclassification
- **Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 20 November 2026 | *Journal of Clinical Epidemiology* | Academic methodology
- **Summary:** Researchers evaluated observational studies that reported protective effects of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) against cancer while purporting to apply target trial emulation (TTE). The authors noted that several studies required patients to receive at least two prescriptions to qualify as exposed, yet initiated person-time follow-up at the first prescription. Through Monte Carlo simulations (500 replications, n = 5,000 per replication) under a true null effect (hazard ratio [HR] = 1.0), reproducing this design generated artifactual protective HRs ranging from 0.72 down to 0.39 in longer follow-up settings (and 0.85 to 0.60 in shorter follow-up). Enforcing strict alignment of eligibility, treatment assignment, and start of follow-up (time zero, EAT0) restored the true null effect.
- **Why it matters:** Invoking "target trial emulation" terminology does not guarantee validity. Conditioning exposure classification on events occurring after baseline without adjusting the index date induces immortal time bias through both exposure misclassification and survivor selection, generating profound false-positive protective signals.
- **Decision affected:** Observational comparative effectiveness protocol design, RWE submission validation by HTA and regulatory bodies, and critical appraisal of published pharmacoepidemiological literature.
- **Source:** [Journal of Clinical Epidemiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43064262/)

### Selection of Primary Marginal Estimators in External Comparator Studies (ATE, ATT, ATU, ATO)
- **Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 16 November 2026 | *Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety* | Methodology
- **Summary:** This study examined trade-offs in selecting marginal estimators—Average Treatment Effect (ATE), Average Treatment Effect in the Treated (ATT), Average Treatment Effect in the Untreated (ATU), and Average Treatment Effect in the Overlap Population (ATO)—for single-arm trials (SATs) benchmarked against external comparators (ECs). While ATT and ATE are traditionally selected, the authors argue that all four estimators provide complementary insight and should be reported. Notably, they highlight ATU as a valid and often superior primary candidate because it remains unbiased under the typical selection scenario where real-world external comparator cohorts include broader, less selective patient populations than the trial cohort.
- **Why it matters:** Regulatory and HTA guidance documents frequently demand pre-specification of primary estimators for propensity-score weighted external control studies without providing guidance on marginal target populations. Choosing between ATT, ATE, ATU, or ATO directly dictates the target population to which findings apply and governs susceptibility to positivity violations.
- **Decision affected:** Statistical analysis plans (SAPs) for external control arm submissions, synthetic control design, and HTA indirect treatment comparison submissions.
- **Source:** [Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety](https://doi.org/10.1002/pds.70523)

### RADISH: Covariate-Adaptive Dynamic Borrowing of Historical Controls in Clinical Trials
- **Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 15 November 2026 | arXiv | Statistical methodology
- **Summary:** The authors propose RADISH (*Robust Adaptive Discrepancy-Informed Shrinkage for Historical Borrowing*), a trial design framework that evaluates compatibility between historical and concurrent control participants at the individual covariate level rather than the aggregate cohort level. RADISH incorporates this adaptive shrinkage directly into sequential randomization and final inference. The resulting estimator is asymptotically normal and supports valid Wald inference. In simulations using data from two real-world randomized trials, RADISH dynamically reduced sample size requirements for concurrent controls when populations were compatible, while progressively attenuating borrowing toward a standard concurrent-only trial as covariate incompatibility increased.
- **Why it matters:** Standard dynamic borrowing approaches apply population-level borrowing parameters, risking severe type I error inflation or bias if unmeasured covariate drift exists between eras. Covariate-adaptive individual-level shrinkage safeguards trial integrity while reducing concurrent control sample requirements.
- **Decision affected:** Adaptive platform trial designs, hybrid trial protocols incorporating RWD or historical control arms, and rare disease trial sample-size optimization.
- **Caveat:** Preprint (not peer reviewed).
- **Source:** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19968v1)

### Design-Based Causal Inference for Fuzzy Difference-in-Differences
- **Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 18 November 2026 | arXiv | Econometrics / Causal inference
- **Summary:** This paper formulates fuzzy difference-in-differences (DiD) designs under a design-based causal inference framework where potential outcomes are treated as fixed and policy exposure assignment is random. ### Clarifying the Target Bias of Test-Negative Designs in Pharmacoepidemiology
- **Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 19 November 2026 | *Epidemiology* | Pharmacoepidemiological methodology
- **Summary:** A methodological analysis clarified the structural causal mechanism underlying the test-negative design (TND).
