**Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 12 November 2026 (RFI issued) / 16 November 2026 (reported) | US FDA (CDER/CBER) | United States
**Summary:** The US FDA published a Request for Information (RFI) seeking public input on overcoming data privacy, governance, and contractual obstacles when submitting patient-level real-world data (RWD) for drug and biological product applications. Historically, the agency has required individual patient-level data to independently verify study endpoints. However, health privacy laws such as HIPAA in the United States and GDPR in the European Union, alongside third-party data ownership, frequently restrict sponsor access to fully identifiable or patient-level records. The RFI requests feedback on acceptable de-identification methods, data provenance standards, contractual arrangements, and secure analytics environments. Public comments close on 11 January 2027.
**Why it matters:** Verification of raw patient-level data remains one of the primary friction points in regulatory submissions incorporating EHR, registry, or claims data. Establishing standardized, privacy-preserving validation pathways could lower regulatory submission hurdles for global real-world evidence (RWE) packages.
**Decision affected:** RWD governance, submission dossier preparation, sponsor-vendor contracting, data anonymization validation.
**Caveat:** The RFI is an information-gathering exercise and does not establish binding regulatory policy or guarantee immediate changes to FDA submission requirements.

### Meta-Research Reveals Regulatory Alignment and Rigor Deficits in Vaccine Target Trial Emulations
**Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 20 November 2026 | JAMA Network Open | Global
**Summary:** A systematic evaluation published in JAMA Network Open examined 79 peer-reviewed observational studies (2021–2025) that applied Target Trial Emulation (TTE) to evaluate vaccine effectiveness or safety using clinical data. The review assessed adherence to core TTE design components and alignment with regulatory RWE standards. Only 25 of 79 studies (31.6%) adhered completely to core TTE design principles, with frequent omissions in explicit time-zero alignment, treatment strategy specification, and pre-specified protocol reporting.
**Why it matters:** Although TTE is widely cited as a benchmark methodology to eliminate immortal time and selection biases in observational data, superficial application threatens the credibility of observational vaccine research submitted to regulatory and public health bodies.
**Decision affected:** Observational vaccine study protocols, RWE dossier appraisal, systematic review methodology.
**Source:** [JAMA Network Open](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.43921)

### Methodological Limits of Unanchored Matching-Adjusted Indirect Comparisons (MAICs)
**Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 19 November 2026 | Value in Health | Global
**Summary:** A methodological article in Value in Health evaluates the boundaries between comparative observational evidence and causal inference when applying unanchored Matching-Adjusted Indirect Comparisons (MAICs). The analysis details how unanchored MAICs rely on strict, non-testable assumptions that all purely prognostic factors and effect modifiers are measured and perfectly adjusted across non-overlapping trial populations. The authors argue that unanchored MAICs should be interpreted as exploratory causal models rather than direct trial comparisons.
**Why it matters:** Unanchored MAICs are frequently submitted to HTA agencies when no common comparator arm exists. Explicitly delineating where statistical matching ends and unverifiable causal assumptions begin provides HTA committees with clearer frameworks to assess residual confounding risk.
**Decision affected:** HTA evidence synthesis, indirect treatment comparison submissions, single-arm trial benchmarking.
**Source:** [Value in Health](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2026.11.002)

### Assessing Distributional Covariate Balance Beyond Standard Mean Differences in Weighted Observational Studies
**Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 16 November 2026 | arXiv (preprint) | Global
**Summary:** Methodological guidance published on arXiv provides a practical framework for applying Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS), Anderson-Darling (AD), and Cramer-von Mises (CVM) statistics as distributional balance diagnostics following propensity score weighting. Demonstrating on an empirical dataset, the authors showed that relying solely on standardized mean differences (SMDs < 0.10) can obscure substantial residual imbalances in covariate higher moments and tails. Incorporating non-linear terms into propensity models eliminated residual distributional differences detected by KS permutation tests without destabilizing overall effect estimates.
**Why it matters:** Regulatory and HTA guidance documents increasingly require rigorous balance reporting in propensity-score matched or weighted RWE studies. Incorporating full distributional diagnostics guards against residual confounding in skewed continuous variables.
**Decision affected:** Propensity score model specification, balance diagnostic protocol design, sensitivity analysis.
**Caveat:** Preprint (not peer reviewed).
**Source:** [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21564v1)
