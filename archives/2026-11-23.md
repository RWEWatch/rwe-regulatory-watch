### Target Trial Emulation Pitfalls: Post-Baseline Exposure Definitions Induce Severe Immortal Time Bias
- **Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 20 November 2026 / *Journal of Clinical Epidemiology* / International
- **Summary:** Established fact: A methodological study evaluated observational studies that cite Target Trial Emulation (TTE) while defining exposure using post-baseline information (e.g., $\ge 2$ prescriptions) while initiating follow-up at the first prescription. Monte Carlo simulations (500 replications, n=5,000 per replication) under a true null effect (hazard ratio = 1.0) reproduced published GLP-1 receptor agonist and cancer outcome hazard ratios ranging from 0.72 to 0.39 (longer follow-up) and 0.85 to 0.60 (shorter follow-up). Correctly aligning eligibility, treatment assignment, and time zero completely eliminated this spurious survival benefit.
- **Why it matters:** Invoking "Target Trial Emulation" does not eliminate immortal time bias if eligibility, treatment assignment, and follow-up start (EAT0) are misaligned. Studies relying on post-baseline exposure definitions systematically misclassify exposure and select survivors, generating biased protective treatment effects.
- **Decision affected:** Observational protocol design, causal inference validation, peer review, and regulatory appraisal of RWE.
- **Source:** [Journal of Clinical Epidemiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43064262/)

### Exposure-Stratified Probabilistic Bias Analysis Required for Non-Differential Outcome Misclassification
- **Date / organisation / jurisdiction:** 16 November 2026 / *European Journal of Epidemiology* / International
- **Summary:** Established fact: A methodological paper demonstrated that positive and negative predictive values (PPV/NPV) depend on underlying true outcome prevalence and vary across exposure strata even when sensitivity and specificity are non-differential. Applying unstratified predictive values in Quantitative Bias Analysis (QBA) introduces bias and incorrectly implies differential misclassification. Using simulations and an applied example (passive smoking and breast cancer), authors showed that exposure-stratified trapezoidal distributions yield unbiased adjusted estimates.
- **Why it matters:** QBA protocols for observational studies must stratify predictive value parameters by exposure status to avoid introducing secondary bias during sensitivity analyses.
- **Decision affected:** Quantitative bias analysis design, bias adjustment protocols, and pharmacoepidemiology sensitivity analysis.
- **Source:** [European Journal of Epidemiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43037921/)
